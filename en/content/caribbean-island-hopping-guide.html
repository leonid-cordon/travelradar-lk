Caribbean Island Hopping Guide: Ferries, Regional Flights and Routes That Actually Work

The Caribbean is not Greece: no single ferry network links the basin, and a bad island pairing routes you back through Miami. Here are the clusters that actually work.

Most travelers picture Caribbean island hopping like Greece: buy a deck ticket at the pier, cross calm water in 45 minutes, walk into a new town. That breaks fast here. More than two dozen territories sit behind separate customs booths, currencies and airline hubs built to feed Miami rather than each other.

Multi-island trips still work smoothly when you pick the cluster before the wish list. If you are still choosing destinations by beach or budget, read our Caribbean island comparison first. This guide maps the ferry chains and short prop hops that work, the cross-basin pairings that burn a full day, and when a cruise beats DIY logistics.

Quick Answer: Where Caribbean Island Hopping Actually Works

Treat the Caribbean as tight local clusters and cap a 7-day round-trip itinerary at two overnight islands (a third base only works smoothly on a one-way open-jaw flight).

  • Easiest ferry cluster: USVI + BVI (St. Thomas, St. John, Tortola, Virgin Gorda) — 20–60 min crossings in sheltered channels.
  • Best luxury & beach hops: St. Maarten (SXM) + Anguilla (20–25 min) + St. Barths (45–60 min ferry or 10-min flight).
  • Best long ferry chain: Guadeloupe – Dominica – Martinique – Saint Lucia on FRS Express des Îles (1.5–2.5 hrs/leg).
  • Best short-flight cluster: Aruba, Curaçao and Bonaire (no ferries; 20–50 min twin-prop hops, outside hurricane belt).
  • Biggest trap: Pairing distant islands like Jamaica + Aruba — expect a 7–11 hr detour via Miami or Panama.
Geography first: Avoid building a Caribbean itinerary from a zoomed-out map. Two islands 500 miles apart with no nonstop service often take longer and cost more to connect than your flight down from the U.S. mainland.

Best Caribbean Island-Hopping Routes for 7 Days

If you want a ready-made starting point rather than a transport lecture, these three sample 7-day sequences match real ferry logic and protect your flight home against boat delays (always align exact nights with your travel week's sailing days):

USVI + BVI (7 Days)

St. John (3 nights) → Tortola with a Virgin Gorda day trip (3 nights) → St. Thomas (1 final night). Fly into STT, take the 20-minute Red Hook boat straight to St. John, cross to Tortola mid-week, and sleep near St. Thomas Airport the last night.

St. Maarten + Anguilla + St. Barths (7 Days)

St. Maarten (2 nights, with a St. Barths day trip) → Anguilla (4 nights) → St. Maarten (1 final night). You get all three islands while packing and unpacking only twice, leaving your departure morning stress-free near SXM.

Guadeloupe + Dominica + Martinique (7 Days · Open-Jaw Exception)

Sample split: Guadeloupe (2 nights) → Dominica (2 nights) → Martinique (3 nights). Book an open-jaw flight (into PTP, home from FDF) and buy separate one-way FRS Express des Îles ferry tickets heading south. It is the rare case where three overnight bases fit a week because you never backtrack — just match your dates to FRS's 3–7 weekly seasonal sailings before locking hotels.

The 4 Island-Hopping Clusters That Actually Work

Sheltered channels keep crossings predictable; open swells and thin flight schedules turn trips into terminal marathons.

Sheltered channels

USVI + BVI

Land in St. Thomas (STT). Red Hook boats hit St. John in 20 min with zero customs; Road Town Fast Ferry and Native Son reach Tortola in 50–60 min. Why it works: Easiest starting point if you want calm water and frequent U.S. flights.

Maximum contrast

St. Maarten + Anguilla + St. Barths

One flight into SXM unlocks Anguilla (20–25 min from Marigot, ~$30) and St. Barths (45–60 min on Great Bay Express or Voyager, or a 10-min Winair hop). Main draw: Three sharply different islands from one gateway airport.

Volcanic arc

French Antilles

FRS Express des Îles links Guadeloupe, Dominica, Martinique and Saint Lucia (foot passengers only), trimming to ~3 weekly round trips in Jan, Jun and Sep. Right fit: Hikers and food-focused travelers comfortable planning around off-season timetables.

Dry southern belt

ABC Islands

Windy open channels keep passenger ferries off this route, so Divi Divi Air and Winair fly 20–50 min prop hops safely south of the hurricane belt. Seasonal edge: Practical September–October choice when northern routes carry storm risk — see our ABC Islands travel guide.

In the Bahamas, Bahamas Ferries sails Nassau to Harbour Island (~3h 10m) only on select days (~$139 one-way / ~$257 RT). A 25-min flight to North Eleuthera or Exuma costs about the same and skips hours of swell — also see our Cancun vs Bahamas guide.

Why Cross-Basin Hopping Fails: The Miami Detour & Real Clock

Caribbean airspace runs like a comb: teeth shoot north-south from Miami, Atlanta and San Juan, with sparse east-west flights between major islands.

Worked example (money + time): Hopping from Montego Bay (MBJ) to Aruba (AUA) has no regular nonstop; routing via Miami or Panama burns 7–11 hours door-to-door and $380–$700+ one-way. Contrast St. Thomas to Tortola: a 55-minute boat costing roughly $100 RT + $40 in mandatory USVI/BVI port/departure taxes ($140 all-in), done in about three hours door-to-door.

Even on an easy ferry route, an international border turns a 55-minute crossing into a three-hour move:

Hidden Costs, Border Rules and Small-Plane Luggage Caps

What usually surprises tourists is how much border paperwork and cash sits behind a 20-minute ferry ride. Per U.S. Customs and Border Protection, U.S. citizens need no passport to fly to Puerto Rico or the USVI, yet stepping onto a Tortola ferry crosses into a British Overseas Territory — making a valid passport book mandatory both ways.

⚓
Unbundled port & departure taxes ($25–$45 RT) — St. Thomas charges $10 wharfage, BVI collects a $10 environmental levy plus $20 ferry departure tax (and online registration at bviedcard.gov.vg). Leaving Anguilla at Blowing Point runs $28 overnight ($11 day-trip) on top of Marigot's ~$5–€7 fee.
🧳
Twin Otter luggage caps (18–20 kg / 39–44 lb) — Divi Divi Air DHC-6 flights cap standard checked bags at 18 kg (39 lb) and cabin bags at 3–7 kg (10×10×14 in). Heavy hard-shell rollers trigger ~$3/kg fees or get bumped to a later flight.
🚕
Airport-to-dock taxi gap ($30–$80 extra) — St. Maarten's airport (SXM) is Dutch-side, while the $30 Anguilla public ferry leaves from French Marigot (20–35 min taxi). Airport-dock shuttles skip the cab but cost ~$55–$75+ one-way.
⏰
Separate-ticket delay risk ($300+ rebooking) — Ferries and regional hops rarely share a ticket with your mainline flight home. If wind cancels a morning boat, your U.S. carrier owes you zero free rebooking.
Carry small USD bills: Card readers at smaller ferry booths go offline often enough to slow down boarding. Keeping $50–$80 per person in cash (plus a few euros in French ports) covers departure taxes and dock taxis cleanly.

Ferry Cluster vs Regional Flights vs Cruise

Independent travelers often dismiss cruises out of habit, yet Caribbean math shifts once you want four or more islands. A 7-night Southern Caribbean cruise from San Juan (SJU) hits St. Thomas, St. Maarten, Saint Lucia, Barbados and Antigua overnight — no 5 a.m. checkouts, no $350 regional fares, no baggage scales. You give up island evenings for 7–9 daytime port hours, which works well if you want a wide sampler. Crossing multiple island telecom zones or sea days? DIY Ferries

2–3 neighbors with real evenings

→ USVI + BVI or St. Maarten + Anguilla/St. Barths ($30–$100 crossings). Skip if choppy water bothers you. Bottom line: Strong balance of quiet evenings and modest transit spend.

Regional Flights

ABC Islands or Bahamas Out Islands

→ Useful where open ocean kills ferry practicality. Plan around $100–$180/leg and bags under 39 lb. Key gain: Reliable autumn weather (ABC) without 3-hour swells.

San Juan Cruise

4–5 islands in one week

→ Sail from SJU instead of Miami to trim sea days and wake in a new port nearly daily. Skip if 4:30 p.m. curfews feel cramped. Value edge: Lowest cost and friction past three stops.

Multi-Region Island

Two vibes, zero boats

→ Split nights across two coasts of Puerto Rico, Jamaica or the Dominican Republic by road. Practical win: Easiest move for families carrying strollers and full suitcases.

5 Rules That Keep Multi-Island Logistics Intact

Ruined itineraries usually come from tight connections with zero buffer:

1
Sleep on your departure-airport island the last night. Never rely on a morning Tortola or Anguilla boat to catch a same-day flight from STT or SXM.
2
Pack soft duffels under 38 lb (17 kg) for prop planes. They fit Twin Otter nose holds and stay under Divi Divi Air and Winair caps.
3
Sit low and aft on open-channel boats. The St. Maarten–St. Barths run gets rough in winter trade winds; rear seats and early motion meds save the day.
4
Check Sunday and September sailings before booking hotels. Ferries cut frequency on Sundays, holidays and Aug–Oct dry-dock windows.
5
Verify entry rules for every territory. Check U.S. State Department advisories and ensure your insurance covers separate-ticket delays.

4 Island-Hopping Mistakes to Avoid

A large share of Caribbean transit complaints trace back to one of these four shortcuts:

Mistake 01

Pairing non-adjacent islands in one week. Jamaica + Aruba or Bahamas + Saint Lucia burns a full day in hub airports and adds $400–$700+ per person. If Jamaica is on your list, pair two coasts on the island itself instead.

Mistake 02

Arriving in the USVI without a passport, then trying to visit the BVI. A U.S. ID gets you to St. John, never to Tortola or Virgin Gorda.

Mistake 03

Packing 3+ overnight islands into a seven-day round-trip itinerary. Two border-crossing move days plus backtracking leave you checking in tired right as afternoon clouds build. Stop at two bases unless you fly open-jaw one way.

Mistake 04

Trusting cached online timetables blindly. Swell and inspections shift boats on short notice; reconfirm pier and departure time directly 24 hours ahead.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Caribbean islands are easiest to island hop by ferry? +

The two smoothest ferry clusters are the U.S. and British Virgin Islands (St. Thomas, St. John, Tortola and Virgin Gorda) and the St. Maarten hub (20-25 minutes to Anguilla, 45-60 minutes to St. Barths). Further south, FRS Express des Iles links Guadeloupe, Dominica, Martinique and Saint Lucia, though crossings take 1.5 to 2.5 hours and drop to roughly three weekly round trips in low months.

Can you island hop in the Caribbean without flying through Miami? +

Yes, if you stay inside one sub-region. Ferries serve the Virgin Islands, St. Maarten's neighbors and the French Antilles, while Divi Divi Air, Winair and interCaribbean fly direct local hops. You get pushed north to Miami or Panama mainly when pairing distant islands across basins, such as Jamaica and Aruba.

Is Caribbean island hopping cheaper on your own or on a cruise? +

For four or five islands in seven days, a Southern Caribbean cruise out of San Juan is almost always cheaper and less tiring than buying regional flights, port taxes and hotel transfers separately. DIY hopping wins on value and depth only when you limit yourself to two or three neighboring islands with at least three nights per base.

Do US citizens need a passport to take a ferry from the USVI to the BVI? +

Yes. U.S. citizens do not need a passport to fly domestically to St. Thomas, but boarding a ferry to Tortola or Virgin Gorda crosses an international border into a British Overseas Territory. You need a valid passport book for BVI immigration and U.S. CBP re-entry, plus the online BVI ED Card before arrival.

How many Caribbean islands should you visit in one week? +

Two islands for most independent round-trip itineraries, or one main base plus one ferry day trip. Each cross-border move eats three to five hours door-to-door once you add checkout, terminal cut-offs, immigration and taxis. Three overnight bases can work in seven days only on a one-way open-jaw flight where you never backtrack; on a round-trip ticket, squeezing in a third base burns too much of the trip in terminals.

Final verdict

Let geography pick your Caribbean route, not a zoomed-out map.

Choose a DIY ferry cluster (USVI + BVI or St. Maarten + Anguilla/St. Barths) for two neighboring islands with real evenings, or the ABC Islands by short prop flight in September–October outside the hurricane belt.

Choose a San Juan cruise if you want four or five islands in one week — or stay on one large multi-region island if border queues and ferry schedules feel like work.
