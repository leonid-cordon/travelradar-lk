Best eSIM for a Caribbean Cruise: Ports, Sea Days & Island Hopping (2026)

One Caribbean week can take you through a U.S. territory, a Dutch island, a French collectivity and an independent country, each on its own network, with sea days in between when a shore-based eSIM has no signal. The right setup depends on your route.

A local SIM makes sense when you land in one country and stay there. On a seven-day cruise or island-hopping loop you cross several carriers in a week, and queuing at SIM kiosks can eat a large part of a port stop. A regional eSIM covers several islands with one install before you leave home.

Two things decide whether it works for you: whether your exact island list is covered, and whether you need a signal on sea days. Regular land eSIMs lose signal once the ship sails beyond coastal tower range, and ship Wi-Fi stops working the moment you step off the gangway. Maritime cruise eSIMs that connect to a ship’s onboard cellular network now close that gap: GigSky was first, Red Bull MOBILE Maritime covers Telenor-equipped vessels, and Saily launched its own cruise plans in June 2026. The choice is no longer “land eSIM or expensive Wi-Fi.”

Quick Answer: Which Caribbean eSIM Fits Your Trip?

For most cruisers, the cleanest setup is a Saily Caribbean Islands bundle for port days (3 GB for $20.99 or 5 GB for $33.99, both valid 30 days), with Airplane Mode on between ports. It has the widest regional island list we checked (29 destinations) and explicitly includes both the BVI and Dutch Sint Maarten. If you also want light messaging and maps on sea days, either pair Saily Caribbean with Saily Cruise (sea-only, from $16.99) or pick GigSky Cruise + Land to keep sea and port data on a single eSIM. A week of ship Wi-Fi runs $143–$280, so eSIMs save real money unless you need video calls or remote work.

Airalo vs Yesim vs Saily vs GigSky: How They Differ

Every land plan promises 4G or 5G on the islands. The real differences sit in the island lists, validity windows, Fair Use Policies and what happens once the ship leaves port.

Airalo Island Hopper

Airalo’s page lists 26 entries, including Aruba, the Bahamas, Barbados, Bermuda, Bonaire, Curaçao, Jamaica, Puerto Rico, Saint Lucia, French Saint Martin and Turks & Caicos, plus a few mainland entries (Panama, Guyana, French Guiana). The plan is data-only, allows hotspot sharing, starts counting validity upon first connecting in its coverage area, and tops up cleanly in the app. Fixed bundles start at $8 for 1 GB (7 days); Airalo also sells 3, 5 and 10 GB fixed tiers at per-GB pricing broadly comparable to Saily’s (secondary aggregators list 5 GB / 30 days around $32.50–$33.99 — check the live price in the app).

Airalo’s clearest strength is its unlimited lineup: $45 for 7 days and $149 for 30 days, matching Holafly and sharply undercutting Yesim. Note that Airalo’s unlimited packages enforce a daily high-speed Fair Use Policy (widely reported by eSIM trackers at 3 GB/day at full speed before throttling to ~1 Mbps until the next 24-hour cycle — confirm current terms in the app). Because Airalo’s public list does not name the BVI, the Dominican Republic, the USVI or Dutch Sint Maarten, verify every stop before buying. It does not work at sea.

Saily Cruise

Launched in June 2026, Saily Cruise connects exclusively to onboard shipboard cellular networks (“901-18,” “WMS,” or “Cellular at Sea”) once the vessel sails more than 12 nautical miles offshore. Plans run from 500 MB for 1 day ($16.99) to 10 GB for 30 days ($109.99), covering 200+ ships across 25+ cruise lines (28 operators in the app, including Royal Caribbean, Carnival, Norwegian, MSC, Celebrity, Princess, Disney and Virgin Voyages, plus several ferry lines). Two practical caveats matter: Saily Cruise does not work in port or within 12 nautical miles of land, so you must buy a separate land eSIM for port days. And because modern iPhones (13 and newer) and Android phones only allow two active SIM/eSIM lines simultaneously, keeping your Home SIM active alongside both Saily Caribbean and Saily Cruise means manually toggling between your land eSIM and cruise eSIM as you enter and leave port.

Yesim Caribbean

Yesim pairs 30-day fixed tiers (€11.80 for 1 GB, €31 for 3 GB, €42 for 5 GB, €58 for 10 GB) with daily unlimited plans at €18 for 1 day and €126 for 7 days. GigSky Cruise + Land

GigSky connects to shipboard maritime cellular networks on more than 290 ships (Royal Caribbean, Carnival, Norwegian, Celebrity, MSC, Princess and Disney among them) and roughly 48–50 land territories across the Americas and Caribbean — including the BVI, the USVI, Puerto Rico, the Dominican Republic and Sint Maarten. A free 100 MB trial requires no credit card, and having both sea and port coverage on one eSIM avoids the two-active-SIM juggling act required when pairing separate sea and land eSIMs.

Two operational details matter. First, installation vs. activation: GigSky starts counting validity the moment the eSIM connects to a covered cellular network. Because the Americas & Caribbean bundle covers the U.S. and Canada, installing it at home and leaving the line switched ON will immediately latch onto your local tower and burn days before you board. Install over home Wi-Fi and immediately toggle the GigSky line OFF (or install on embarkation day) until departure. Second, expect modest speeds at sea: GigSky quotes 700 Kbps to 4 Mbps shared over a satellite backhaul, and ship networks require 4G/LTE enabled (a 5G-only setting will not register). Skip GigSky for hotel-only trips, where its per-GB cost trails Saily and Airalo.

What About Holafly (and Its “Caribbean Cruise” eSIM)?

Holafly sells two confusingly similar products: its standard Caribbean eSIM (18 destinations, $46.90 for 7 days, excluding the Bahamas, Puerto Rico and Sint Maarten) and a separately marketed Caribbean Cruise eSIM (covering port stops such as the USA, Mexico, Bahamas, Dominican Republic, Puerto Rico, Jamaica, Saint Martin, Cayman Islands, Honduras, Panama and Colombia). Crucial warning: neither Holafly plan connects to Cellular at Sea or WMS maritime networks — both work only on land towers in port or near shore, despite “Cruise” in the product name.

Both plans cap hotspot sharing at 1 GB per day of the plan. At $46.90 for 7 days, Holafly costs almost the same as Saily’s 10 GB / 30-day bundle ($46.99) and slightly more than Airalo’s 7-day unlimited Island Hopper ($45), so it makes sense mainly if you expect to exceed 10 GB in port during a single week and verify every port on its roster.

Who Can Skip an eSIM, and Which Islands Break Regional Plans

Before buying a regional eSIM, check whether your existing carrier plan already covers your shore stops — then check the Caribbean telecom splits that trip up travelers.

Skip a port eSIM if: your itinerary stays inside the U.S., Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands on a U.S. postpaid carrier (AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile treat all three as domestic coverage). Three flagship U.S. consumer plans also cover mainstream Caribbean ports at $0 extra: AT&T Elite 2.0 (20 GB/month across 210+ land destinations; $15/day Cruise Day Pass at sea), Verizon Unlimited Ultimate (land data, talk and text in 210+ destinations; $20/day Cruise Daily Pass at sea), and T-Mobile Go5G Plus / Experience More / Magenta MAX (5 GB high-speed in 215+ destinations; 15 GB on Go5G Next / Experience Beyond, then 256 kbps). None of these include free sea-day cellular data — and T-Mobile has no cruise data pass at all.
  • St. Martin and Sint Maarten: No physical road checkpoint separates the French and Dutch halves, yet they run separate telecom regimes. Princess Juliana Airport (SXM) and the cruise pier sit on the Dutch side; the French side is licensed to Orange, Dauphin Telecom, Digicel and UTS Caraïbe (EU roaming). Near the border, phones routinely cross-roam onto the opposite side. Saily and GigSky cover both halves; Airalo’s Island Hopper names French Saint Martin only.
  • St. Thomas / St. John to Tortola (BVI): Domestic U.S. coverage ends once you cross into the British Virgin Islands (roughly 20–40 minutes by ferry from Red Hook to West End, or 45–60 minutes from Charlotte Amalie to Road Town; see our Caribbean island hopping guide). Even St. John’s north shore can pull a BVI signal and trigger a $12 carrier pass. Saily, Holafly and GigSky explicitly name the BVI; Airalo’s public list does not.
  • Dominican Republic, Bahamas and USVI: The biggest coverage divergences sit right here. The Dominican Republic appears on GigSky and Holafly’s lists, but not on Airalo Island Hopper or Saily Caribbean Islands. The Bahamas are covered by Airalo, Saily and GigSky, but omitted from Holafly’s standard Caribbean bundle (included on Holafly’s Caribbean Cruise port plan). The USVI are explicitly named by GigSky.
  • Cuba: Served solely by state monopoly ETECSA (Cubacel) and excluded from Airalo, Saily, Yesim and Holafly regional bundles. Buy a dedicated Cuba eSIM and expect slower speeds.
  • Aruba, Bonaire and Curaçao (plus Jamaica): Aruba and Curaçao appear on all main lists (covered in our ABC Islands travel guide), while Bonaire is listed by Airalo and Saily but omitted by Holafly. Resort corridors in Jamaica hold dependable 4G/LTE on regional eSIMs, with ordinary indoor or hillside dips.

Cruise Ship Wi-Fi vs Port eSIM vs Cellular at Sea

One cruise afternoon moves your phone through four distinct connectivity zones — plus two physical realities most guides omit.

  • Docked in standard ports (Nassau, San Juan, Philipsburg, Cozumel): The ship switches off its maritime cellular transmitter near shore, and a regional eSIM covering that island connects to local towers normally.
  • Sailing out of coastal range: Shore signal fades gradually depending on tower distance, coastline and deck position. Shipboard cellular networks — miniature cell towers mounted on the vessel and backhauled to shore via satellite (Cellular at Sea, “wmsatsea,” or “901-18”) — activate once the ship enters international waters (AT&T cites 6+ nautical miles offshore; Verizon, GigSky and Saily cite 12 nautical miles). Expect a brief dead zone between the two and roughly 20 minutes of congestion right after the ship’s network turns on. One background app refresh on an unprotected Home SIM can trigger a $15–$20 cruise pass — or steep pay-per-use charges on Bell, Telus or T-Mobile (including $5.99/min on T-Mobile for an unanswered call that rolls to voicemail).
  • Open-ocean sea days (and steel-cabin signal physics): Land eSIMs show “No Service.” Live options narrow to onboard ship Wi-Fi, a carrier cruise pass (Verizon $20/day; AT&T $20/day standard or $15/day Elite 2.0; Rogers CAD $18/day or CAD $100/7-day pass), or a maritime eSIM (GigSky Cruise + Land, Saily Cruise, or Red Bull MOBILE Maritime). Cellular at sea runs on 2G/3G/LTE at roughly 700 Kbps–4 Mbps shared over satellite, and shipboard cellular antennas sit mostly in public decks and hallways. Steel bulkheads in interior cabins and lower decks heavily weaken or block maritime cellular signal, whereas ship Wi-Fi access points are wired into stateroom corridors. Onboard Wi-Fi runs dynamically around $18–$39/day on Royal Caribbean VOOM (averaging near $25–$29 pre-booked); $20.40 (Social), $23.80 (Value) or $25.50 (Premium) pre-cruise on Carnival; and $29.99 (Voyage Wi-Fi) or $39.99 (Streaming) per day on Norwegian.
  • Private cruise islands (2026 rules): Ship Wi-Fi carries ashore onto Perfect Day at CocoCay (Royal Caribbean VOOM), Celebration Key in Grand Bahama (Carnival Wi-Fi and HUB App), Ocean Cay Marine Reserve (MSC active packages), and Castaway Cay (paid Disney Cruise Line packages; no free public Wi-Fi). By contrast, per Norwegian Cruise Line’s official terms, NCL Wi-Fi packages are not available at Great Stirrup Cay (Bahamas) or Harvest Caye (Belize) — plan on cellular roaming or a port eSIM covering the Bahamas or Belize if you need data on the beach.
Free or $5 onboard chat without buying Wi-Fi or sea data: If you only need to coordinate with family or friends on the same ship, put your phone in Airplane Mode, join the ship’s free local Wi-Fi intranet, and use the line’s built-in app chat: Royal Caribbean’s Royal App and Disney’s Navigator App include guest chat for free (Disney’s free intranet also commonly passes text-only iMessage), while Carnival’s HUB App unlocks cruise-long chat for a flat $5 per person.
Cruiser rule: Switch on Airplane Mode the moment the gangway retracts, then manually turn Wi-Fi back on for the free ship app or paid onboard Wi-Fi. Turn cellular back on only once docked — unless you intentionally run a cruise eSIM or carrier cruise pass.

Pre-Departure eSIM Setup Checklist

Downloading an eSIM requires stable Wi-Fi. Finish steps 1–5 at home — never inside a congested cruise terminal or island airport.

Seven Steps Before Boarding

Complete steps 1–5 and step 7 over home Wi-Fi; switch step 6 on arrival.

Confirm your phone is unlocked. On iPhone, open Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock and confirm it reads “No SIM restrictions.” Carrier-financed locked phones reject third-party eSIMs. On Android, verify unlock status in your carrier app.
Enable Wi-Fi Calling and an authenticator app before leaving home. If you plan to use paid ship Wi-Fi in Airplane Mode, turn on Wi-Fi Calling while still on your home domestic network so calls and texts back to U.S./Canadian numbers can route over Wi-Fi (note: calls to non-home international numbers over Wi-Fi Calling can still incur carrier fees, and automated bank 2FA short codes can be unreliable over satellite Wi-Fi — switch key bank logins to an authenticator app before sailing).
Install the eSIM, then toggle the line OFF until needed. Airalo, Saily and Holafly start counting validity on the first connection to a covered network. Because the U.S. or Puerto Rico appear on some regional rosters (including GigSky Americas & Caribbean and Saily’s “United States” entry), leaving the travel eSIM ON at home or in Florida can start your clock days early. Install on home Wi-Fi, turn the eSIM line OFF immediately, and switch it ON only on embarkation day (GigSky) or at your first foreign port (land eSIMs).
Label your lines clearly (“Home” and “Caribbean eSIM”) so you never flip the wrong toggle in an immigration queue.
Lock down your Home SIM: set Home line Data Roaming OFF, disable Allow Cellular Data Switching (iOS) / Auto Data Switching (Android), pause iCloud/Google Photos cellular backups, and ensure 5G-only is not forced if you plan to use a cruise eSIM (ship towers run 2G/3G/LTE).
On arrival in port, set Cellular Data to your Caribbean eSIM and switch Data Roaming ON for that travel eSIM only so it joins local partner towers. Remember that phones support only two active SIMs at once, so if you use both Saily Caribbean and Saily Cruise alongside your Home SIM, toggle off the inactive travel eSIM.
Download offline Google Maps for every island stop over home Wi-Fi as a dockside backup.

Five Caribbean Mobile Data Mistakes Worth Avoiding

Most roaming bill shocks and failed eSIM installs come down to one of these five errors:

1
Buying an eSIM for a carrier-locked phone. Financed U.S. phones frequently stay locked until paid off and will reject third-party eSIM profiles. Verify Carrier Lock before checkout.
2
Leaving Allow Cellular Data Switching on at sea — or ignoring voicemail on T-Mobile. When shore signal fades, your phone can silently fall back onto your Home SIM over Cellular at Sea ($15–$20/day on AT&T/Verizon, or pay-per-use on Bell/Telus/T-Mobile, where even an unanswered call rolling to voicemail on T-Mobile triggers $5.99/min).
3
Buying “Holafly Caribbean Cruise” expecting ocean signal, or assuming one bundle covers every port. Holafly’s Cruise eSIM is land/port-only; Cuba is excluded everywhere; the Dominican Republic and BVI vanish from several lists; French St. Martin and Dutch Sint Maarten are separate telecom zones; and NCL’s Wi-Fi does not extend onto Great Stirrup Cay or Harvest Caye.
4
Switching Data Roaming off globally. Home SIM roaming stays OFF, while your travel eSIM requires Data Roaming ON to connect to local Caribbean partner carriers.
5

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a Caribbean regional eSIM work on a cruise ship at sea? +

Not on open-ocean sea days. Standard plans from Airalo, Yesim, Saily Caribbean Islands and even Holafly's "Caribbean Cruise" eSIM connect only to shore towers, so signal fades gradually as the ship leaves coastal range. Onboard shipboard cellular networks backhauled via satellite (Cellular at Sea / WMS) take over in international waters (about 6+ nautical miles out per AT&T; 12 nautical miles per Verizon, GigSky and Saily). For sea-day cellular data without ship Wi-Fi, choose GigSky Cruise + Land (ship and port), Saily Cruise (sea-only), or Red Bull MOBILE Maritime on Telenor-equipped ships; otherwise keep Airplane Mode on between ports.

Which is better for island hopping: Airalo, Yesim or Saily? +

Check the island list first. Saily covers 29 destinations -- including the BVI and Dutch Sint Maarten -- with clear fixed tiers ($8.99 for 1 GB/7d; $20.99, $33.99 and $46.99 for 3, 5 and 10 GB/30d). Airalo Island Hopper lists 26 entries from $8 (1 GB/7d) and beats Saily if you want unlimited data ($45 for 7 days, subject to daily high-speed FUP), though its list omits the BVI and Dominican Republic. Yesim covers only 14 countries and prices higher (EUR 31 for 3 GB; EUR 126 for 7-day unlimited), making sense mainly if you want one reusable profile across future regions.

Is a Caribbean eSIM cheaper than AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile or Canadian carrier roaming? +

On multi-island trips, usually yes. Standard Verizon and AT&T land passes bill $12/day ($84 over seven land days, plus $20/day at sea), Rogers bills CAD $18/day (CAD $60 for a 7-day land pass), and Bell/Telus run CAD $16-$18/day with no cruise coverage -- compared with $20.99-$33.99 for 3-5 GB on Saily. Skip a port eSIM if your trip stays in Puerto Rico and the USVI on a U.S. plan, or if you already hold AT&T Elite 2.0, Verizon Unlimited Ultimate, or T-Mobile Go5G Plus/Next (on all three, only sea days cost extra).

What about unlimited-only Caribbean eSIMs like Holafly? +

Holafly's standard Caribbean plan covers 18 destinations (excluding the Bahamas, Puerto Rico and Sint Maarten) at $46.90 for 7 days with a 1 GB/day shareable hotspot cap; its separate "Caribbean Cruise" eSIM adds stops like the Bahamas and Mexico but still works in port only, not at sea. Because $46.90 nearly equals Saily's 10 GB / 30-day bundle ($46.99) and Airalo's 7-day unlimited plan ($45), Holafly pays off mainly if you expect to exceed 10 GB ashore in one week.

Will my home number still receive bank 2FA texts with a Caribbean eSIM? +

In port, usually yes: keep your Home line on, assign Cellular Data to the Caribbean eSIM, and turn off Home Data Roaming and Allow Cellular Data Switching (incoming SMS is domestic-rated on AT&T and typically free in port, though Verizon bills $0.05 per received text without a pass at sea, and replying or answering a call triggers roaming). In Airplane Mode at sea, cellular SMS will not arrive -- enabling Wi-Fi Calling before departure lets texts pass over paid ship Wi-Fi on many carriers, though automated bank short codes can still fail, so set up an authenticator app before boarding.

Final verdict

Settle two questions before paying: which islands are actually on your itinerary, and whether you need mobile data once the ship leaves coastal tower range.

For port days and island hopping, buy a 3 GB ($20.99) or 5 GB ($33.99) 30-day Saily Caribbean Islands bundle — our default because its 29-destination roster explicitly covers both the BVI and Dutch Sint Maarten. Choose Airalo Island Hopper if your stops match its 26-entry list and you want a $45 seven-day unlimited package or an $8 starter week. Choose Yesim only if you specifically want a single reusable eSIM across multiple trips.

For light messaging, email and maps on cruise sea days, GigSky Cruise + Land ($34.99–$69.99 list, often discounted) is the smoothest option because one eSIM handles both ship and ports without hitting dual-SIM limits; Saily Cruise (1 GB for $29.99) plus Saily Caribbean ($20.99) gives more total data (~$51 combined) if you don’t mind toggling two eSIMs. Existing AT&T Elite 2.0 customers beat both at $45 for three sea days (ports included), and Rogers customers should look at its CAD $100 seven-day Cruise pass first — while Bell, Telus and T-Mobile cruisers must treat sea days as Airplane Mode + cruise eSIM or ship Wi-Fi.

For live video calls, remote work or reliable interior-cabin coverage, pre-book ship Wi-Fi and pair it with a cheap port eSIM. Shipboard cellular runs at 700 Kbps–4 Mbps and weakens sharply behind steel stateroom walls.
